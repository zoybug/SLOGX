# Digital Transformation of Modern Supply Chains — Fall 2025

**Lecture 01 · 15 September 2025 · Professor Wai Kin (Victor) Chan**

## Lecture context

The lecture links supply-chain history and AI readiness to three operating cases: fresh-food forecasting and procurement, Flexport’s connected trade and carbon measurement, and S.F. Holding’s network planning, digital twins and UAVs. People, planning, shared data and responsibility accompany intelligent technology and automation.

## What the class found

This anonymous synthesis covers **30 Lecture 01 reflections from two supplied submission batches**. A report about another lecture is excluded. This is a corpus count, not a verified enrollment roster. Themes below are paraphrases, not attributed quotations or instructor feedback.

### Q1 — What helped

Practical cases made concepts concrete. History and structure oriented students to the field. Polls and discussion encouraged participation. Readiness and ethics balanced the technological possibilities.

### Q2 — What could improve

Students asked for clearer diagrams, technical primers, deeper selected cases, guided demonstrations, group tasks and accessible prelearning. They also wanted implementation constraints, ROI, skills and smaller-firm contexts. Views differ: some prefer simpler explanations while others ask for mathematics and model detail; pace requests are not unanimous.

### Q4 — Shared direction and differing views

Many reflections describe connected, predictive and adaptive systems. Three flows—physical, information and financial—extend the discussion beyond vehicles and sensors.

Some students imagine extensive autonomy; others emphasize human judgement, empathy, exceptions and phased delegation. Sustainability is often framed as less spoilage or wasted transport, while a distinctive perspective asks about AI’s own resource demand and labor transition. Modular workflows and cross-border coordination make data sharing and handoffs practical design issues.

Distinctive ideas include solving some bottlenecks with basic IT, separating efficiency from service effectiveness, and discussing decision criteria and weights before automating them. These are student perspectives, not verified future forecasts.

## An editorial decision model

**Sense → Connect → Predict → Optimize → Act → Learn**

The model brings the reflections and lecture cases together. It is an editorial teaching model, not a claimed quotation or diagram from the professor. Across the loop, check data quality, objectives, capacity, accountability and outcomes.

| Lecture case | Decision to examine | Suggested outcome checks |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh food | Turn demand estimates into procurement decisions under uncertainty | Service, spoilage and cost against a baseline |
| Flexport | Coordinate bookings, orders and shipment handoffs | Timeliness, utilization, cost and emissions boundary |
| S.F. Holding | Reserve and allocate capacity; test network alternatives | Service stability, resource use and model validity |
