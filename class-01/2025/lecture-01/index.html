2025 STUDENT PERSPECTIVES / 04 OF 05LECTURE 01

Learning and feedback

With the cases and themes in view, consider how students experienced the lecture. Questions 1 and 2 asked what helped them learn and what could improve.

Q1 / WHAT HELPED

Cases made the ideas concrete

  • Fresh food, Flexport and S.F. Holding linked the concepts to practical decisions.
  • The historical overview and clear structure helped students find their way into the subject.
  • Polls and discussion gave more students a way to contribute.
  • Discussion of readiness and ethics helped put the technology in context.

Q2 / WHAT TO IMPROVE

More support and time to explore

  • Use short technical primers, clearer diagrams and case summaries.
  • Add guided tasks, group problems and more detailed case studies.
  • Provide accessible preparation materials and alternatives to restricted demos.
  • Discuss return on investment, smaller firms, skills and implementation challenges.
Views differ. Some students wanted simpler explanations; others wanted more mathematics and model detail. Requests about pace also differed. A short introduction followed by optional deeper material could support both needs.

These themes summarize the reflections; they do not represent a satisfaction score or a unanimous view.

2025 STUDENT PERSPECTIVES / 05 OF 05LECTURE 01

Future possibilities

Question 4 asked students to imagine future logistics. Their responses share some ambitions but differ on how far automation should go and what progress should mean.

Q4 / SHARED DIRECTION

Share information and adapt plans

Many students imagine systems that share information, anticipate demand or disruption and adjust their plans. They also connect the movement of goods with information and payments.

SEE → DECIDE → ACT → REVIEW
A / AUTONOMY

How much should be automated?

Some imagine a fully automated supply chain. Others want human judgment and empathy for exceptions, with responsibility delegated gradually.

B / SUSTAINABILITY

What resources does the system use?

Less spoilage and fewer wasted trips matter. One reflection also asks about AI’s resource use and the effects of changing jobs.

C / INTEGRATION

How will partners work together?

Modular tools and cross-border services still need compatible data and clear handoffs between partners.

D / PURPOSE

What does success mean?

A low-cost plan may miss the service goal. Agree on the outcome and the importance of competing objectives before automating decisions.
