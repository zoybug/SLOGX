Digital Transformation of Modern Supply Chains

Prof. Wai Kin (Victor) Chan connects supply-chain history and AI readiness with three cases: fresh-food procurement, Flexport’s connected trade, and S.F. Holding’s network intelligence. The question throughout is how data becomes a useful, responsible operating decision.

2025 STUDENT CONTENT / 01 OF 05LECTURE 01

Learning responses

Q1 and Q2 show what made the lecture useful and where students want more support.

Q1 / WHAT HELPED

Cases made the field concrete.

  • Fresh food, Flexport and SF connected concepts to operating decisions.
  • The historical storyline and clear structure helped orient a new field.
  • Polls and discussion gave more students a way to contribute.
  • Readiness and ethics balanced the technology examples.

Q2 / WHAT TO IMPROVE

Make space for the next question.

  • Use short technical primers, clearer diagrams and case summaries.
  • Add guided tasks, group problems and deeper selected cases.
  • Offer accessible prelearning and alternatives to restricted demos.
  • Discuss ROI, smaller firms, skills and implementation constraints.
Views differ. Some reflections ask for simpler explanations; others ask for mathematics and model-level depth. Pace requests also differ. A brief explanation with an optional deeper reading can address both.

Q1 and Q2 are qualitative themes from the supplied reflections, not satisfaction scores or a unanimous ranking.

Outlook and differences

Q4 combines a shared direction with different views of autonomy, adoption and value.

Q4 / SHARED DIRECTION

Connected, predictive and adaptive.

Students repeatedly describe systems that share observations, anticipate demand or disruption, adjust plans and act. Physical, information and financial flows broaden the picture beyond vehicles and sensors.

SEE → DECIDE → ACT → REVIEW
A / AUTONOMY

Different limits of delegation

Some imagine a fully automated chain. Others emphasize judgement, empathy, exceptions and phased adoption.

B / SUSTAINABILITY

Widen the system boundary

Less spoilage and fewer wasted trips matter. A distinctive reflection also asks about AI’s resource demand and labor transition.

C / INTEGRATION

Make connections usable

Modular workflows and cross-border coordination require shared data and clear handoffs between partners.

D / PURPOSE

Choose the service goal

Efficiency, effectiveness and weighted objectives can lead to different plans. Define success before automating it.
