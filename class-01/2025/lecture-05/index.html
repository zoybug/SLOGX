High-Precision Positioning Technology and Smart Logistics

How does a vehicle estimate where it is when signals and maps are imperfect? In “High Accuracy Positioning for ITS,” Prof. Kai Zhang connects sensor measurements, state estimation and road matching for intelligent transportation systems (ITS).

PROFESSOR KAI ZHANG · TSINGHUA SIGS20 OCTOBER 2025

Course instructor: Prof. Wai Kin (Victor) Chan. Students wrote a one-page reflection on what interested them most. This page draws on 30 identified reflections and a separate essay with unresolved authorship.

01 / OVERVIEW

Start with the application

Navigation, fleet tracking, emergency response, bus arrivals and road pricing all need position data suited to their task.

02 / MULTISENSOR FUSION

Combine complementary measurements

Satellite positioning can lose signals or receive reflections; inertial estimates can drift. Calibration and an error model help combine the two.

03 / MAP MATCHING

Match observations to a road

Hidden Markov models (HMMs) and Viterbi inference compare possible road sequences. Fréchet methods compare curves. Noisy samples and imperfect maps complicate both approaches.

DECISION + CHECK

Satellite and inertial measurements have different limits

A global navigation satellite system (GNSS) provides an external reference but can suffer blockage and reflected signals. An inertial navigation system (INS) uses acceleration and rotation to estimate motion from a starting state; errors accumulate. Combining them helps address their different weaknesses.

Connect the coordinate with the road

Road geometry, direction, intersections, tunnels and traffic rules help interpret latitude and longitude. An outdated or incorrect map can lead to a wrong match.

Identify the traveled road before planning the next route. Map matching estimates which road was traveled. Route planning chooses a future journey. Positioning can support dispatch and arrival-time estimates, which still need their own models.

Matching the road

Once a position is estimated, several nearby roads may remain plausible. Compare matching each observation separately with matching their sequence.

Three noisy measurements lie near two roads. The nearest-road method considers each measurement alone; this simplified HMM also checks allowed transitions.

NEAREST ROAD

HMM / VITERBI SEQUENCE

Constructed retrospective model. The model uses three samples, two road candidates per sample, equal starting probabilities and Gaussian distance likelihoods. With connectors, the probability of staying is 0.9 and changing is 0.1; without connectors, they are 1 and 0. Viterbi selects the highest-scoring sequence using log likelihoods and backpointers. Speed, heading, route distance and elevation are omitted. The result follows these assumptions; no true trajectory is supplied for validation.

Combine observations with feasible movement

In an HMM, emission probabilities describe how well a measurement fits a road candidate; transition probabilities describe movement between candidates. The lecture also considers heading, distance and speed. Removing unlikely candidates saves computation but can remove the correct road if thresholds or map data are wrong.

Consider when the result is needed

Online and offline methods differ in when they produce a result. Incremental and global methods differ in how much of the trace they use. Later measurements can revise an earlier match in a full-trace analysis; a live system has to work with the evidence available at the time.

Student questions and connections

The filter and road examples give concrete starting points. Students also found useful analogies, raised technical questions and connected the methods with their experience.

01 / GEOMETRIC INTUITION

Use the leash analogy to understand curve matching

Several students found the dog-and-owner analogy helpful for Fréchet distance. It finds the smallest possible maximum separation as both move forward along their curves. They may move at different speeds, so the comparison preserves traversal order rather than the original timestamps.

02 / FORMULAS + LIMITS

Check the error model

One reflection works through covariance and state-estimation equations. The separate essay examines bias, scale factors and axis alignment. Both point to calibration and uncertainty assumptions: a small modeled variance needs validation against the actual position.

03 / LEARNING PACE

Build from intuition to mathematics

Some students connect the methods with mapping experience or future study. One found the lecture dense and wanted a clearer understanding of the Kalman filter. The simple examples provide a way to begin before returning to the mathematics.

04 / DEPLOYMENT CHOICES

Choose an architecture for the application

Students ask about sensor cost, calibration, integration complexity and infrastructure. They also explore ultra-wideband (UWB), real-time kinematic GNSS (RTK) and other sensors as extensions. Their performance would need testing in the intended setting.

Similar curves can remain ambiguous

Similar road shapes may produce equally good geometric matches. The lecture uses free-space diagrams and threshold searches to examine curve similarity. Comparing methods still requires a defined dataset and task.

Separate communication from positioning

One student draws on satellite-communications experience. A data link carries information; GNSS observations help estimate location. A service needs to test connectivity, navigation interference and positioning accuracy separately.

2025 STUDENT PERSPECTIVES / 05 OF 05LECTURE 05

Evaluate the evidence

The application determines how accurate, timely and available an estimate needs to be. Use those requirements to choose the evaluation.

FLEETS + PASSENGERS

Define the decision that needs a position

Fleet tracking, dispatch, emergency response and bus arrivals need different tolerances and update times. Compare the effect of better positioning with matched operating data before claiming time or fuel savings.

ROAD PRICING

Distinguish capability from use

The lecture considers distance-based road charging. The LTA’s October 2023 ERP 2.0 announcement described GNSS capability while retaining the charging framework and stating no immediate plan for distance charging. Read that announcement as historical context, rather than evidence that all journeys were charged by distance.

POSITION + MAP

Specify what accuracy means

Identifying the correct road link does not establish a lane-level position. Measure physical error, road-match correctness, availability, continuity and delay. Test tunnels, reflected signals, sparse samples and changed roads separately.

UNCERTAINTY + VALIDATION

Compare with an independent reference

Use a suitable reference trajectory to measure errors, wrong-road choices, outage duration, recovery and computation delay. Check whether the stated uncertainty matches observed errors, including biased inputs.

Read historical examples with their assumptions. The deck’s constellation timelines and positioning errors describe its source context. Claims about optimal filtering or parameter-free matching depend on the method’s assumptions. A study’s accuracy applies to its stated equipment, data and conditions.
