High-Precision Positioning Technology and Smart Logistics

In “High Accuracy Positioning for ITS,” Prof. Kai Zhang follows the path from satellite and motion measurements to state estimation and road-network interpretation. High accuracy comes from coordinating sensors, models and maps.

Location enables a service.

Navigation, probe vehicles, fleet management, emergency response, bus arrival information and road pricing depend on useful position data.

02 / MULTISENSOR FUSION

Combine different weaknesses.

GNSS multipath and interruptions differ from inertial drift. Sensor calibration, state estimation and integration architecture shape how measurements work together.

Infer the traversed road.

HMM/Viterbi methods evaluate candidate sequences. Fréchet-based methods compare ordered curves. Noisy samples, sparse updates and imperfect maps make nearest-road snapping unreliable.

From signals to service

Trace the measurements, models and decisions.

Select a stage. Each layer adds information, but it also introduces assumptions that need checking.

DECISION + CHECK

GNSS and INS fail differently.

GNSS provides an external position reference but can suffer blockage and reflected signals. INS propagates motion from an initial state using accelerometers and gyroscopes; biases and other errors accumulate. Complementarity motivates fusion, without promising indefinite accuracy during outages.

A coordinate needs context.

The class connects latitude/longitude with the correct road link, direction and feasible movement. Maps add road geometry, intersections, tunnels and traffic rules. An incorrect or outdated map can constrain an estimate to the wrong place.

Locate before optimizing. Map matching estimates the road already traversed. Route planning selects a future route. A positioning pipeline can support dispatch and ETA models, but it does not replace them.

Model assumptions. One scalar update, measurement model H = 1, independent unbiased Gaussian errors and correctly specified variances. P = predicted σ²; R = measurement σ²; K = P/(P + R); new position = 8 + K(z − 8); new variance = (1 − K)P. Missing data skips the update; a sequential INS prediction may increase uncertainty through process noise. This is not a full navigation filter or an automatic detector of multipath bias.

NEAREST ROAD

HMM / VITERBI SEQUENCE

Constructed retrospective model. Three samples, two road candidates at each sample, equal initial state probabilities and Gaussian distance likelihoods. With connectors: stay probability 0.9, change 0.1; without connectors: stay 1, change 0. Viterbi maximizes the sequence score using log likelihoods and backpointers. Roads and samples are simplified; speed, heading, routing distance and elevation are omitted. The result is model-consistent inference, not ground truth or a recreation of the published research algorithm.

Use the sequence and the network.

The reflections connect emissions with how well a fix fits a candidate, and transitions with feasible movement. The lecture also discusses heading, route distance and speed constraints. Candidate pruning reduces computation but may discard the correct road if thresholds or map data are wrong.

Real-time and global are different axes.

Online/offline concerns when results are needed. Incremental/global concerns how much of a trace is considered. A full-trace example can revise an early match using later evidence; a live system cannot assume future samples are already available.

Different ways to understand

Preserve intuition, technical interests and open questions.

01 / GEOMETRIC INTUITION

The leash preserves order.

The dog-and-owner analogy made Fréchet distance accessible in several reflections. It minimizes the maximum separation over permitted forward traversals of both curves. Standard Fréchet compares shape and traversal order; it allows different speeds and does not preserve original timestamps automatically.

02 / FORMULAS + LIMITS

Precision needs an error model.

One reflection develops covariance and state-estimation equations; the unattributed companion emphasizes bias, scale factors and axis misalignment. Calibration and correct uncertainty assumptions remain necessary. A small reported variance alone is not proof that the estimate is close to the true position.

03 / LEARNING PACE

Intuition and depth need room.

Some reflections connect the methods with mapping work or future ITS study. One describes the lecture as dense and the Kalman filter as not yet understood. The one-update and two-road examples provide concrete starting points before returning to the full mathematics.

04 / DEPLOYMENT CHOICES

Choose an architecture for a task.

Students raise integration complexity, sensor price, calibration and regional infrastructure. Indoor UWB, RTK and additional perception sensors appear as broader extensions. They are not evidence that this lecture demonstrated universal centimeter or sub-centimeter performance.

Geometry can remain ambiguous.

Similar road shapes may produce equally good curve matches. The lecture introduces free-space diagrams and threshold searches; geometric interpretability does not establish a universal advantage over probabilistic methods.

Read Eiter and Mannila’s discrete variant ↗

Connectivity and location serve different roles.

A reflection draws on satellite communications experience. A communication link transports data; GNSS observations support positioning. Communication availability, navigation interference and localization accuracy need separate tests, even when they interact in one service.

2025 STUDENT CONTENT / 05 OF 05LECTURE 05

Applications and evidence

Match the claim to its task and conditions.

FLEETS + PASSENGERS

What decision uses the estimate?

Students connect positioning with fleet visibility, dispatch, emergency response and bus arrivals. Specify the task’s tolerance and update deadline. Better location data can support these decisions; quantify benefits with matched operating data rather than assuming guaranteed fuel or time savings.

ROAD PRICING

Capability is not adoption.

The lecture explores distance-based charging. LTA’s October 2023 ERP 2.0 announcement described GNSS capability while retaining the charging framework and reporting no immediate distance-charging plan. Do not infer from the deck or reflections that Singapore was already charging all journeys by distance.

POSITION + MAP

Define the accuracy claim.

Road-link identification is not automatically lane-level localization. Distinguish physical position error, map-match correctness, availability, continuity and latency. Evaluate tunnels, reflected signals, sparse observations and changed roads separately.

UNCERTAINTY + VALIDATION

Test against independent evidence.

Compare the estimate with an appropriate reference trajectory. Report error distributions, incorrect road choices, outage duration, recovery and computation delay. Check uncertainty calibration and biased inputs rather than treating a clean map display as validation.

Keep the context dated. The deck contains legacy constellation timelines and illustrative positioning errors. Its claims of parameter-free geometric matching and optimal filtering need method-specific assumptions. Regional deployment hopes and future sensor proposals in the reflections are not measured outcomes. A study’s accuracy applies to its stated setup, not every vehicle or warehouse.
