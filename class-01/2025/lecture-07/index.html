Global trade and growth

Smart Supply Chain Transformation – Fueling Global Growth

Lecture 07 · Guest lecture · 3 November 2025 · Lecturer: Prof. Henry Ko

Lecture overview

Henry Ko connected global trade with supply chain management and the practical work of logistics. Beginning with exchange and barter, he introduced the relationships between trade, supply chains and the movement of goods. He then considered how technology platforms create business opportunities and used Flexport to discuss the challenges facing banks, merchants and logistics providers. This summary follows the organizer’s notes; the original poster supplies the event details and broader context.

Lecturer profile

At this November 2025 event, Henry Ko was introduced as Chairman, Greater China at Flexport, a founding member of Flexport Asia and an adjunct professor at City University of Hong Kong. His earlier leadership roles include DHL, Li & Fung and SF Express. The official profile provides further background and may describe roles held after the event.

Guest lecture summary

Trade, supply chains and logistics

Ko began with exchange and barter, then distinguished the coordination of a supply chain from the work of moving goods. This progression places logistics within the wider relationships that make trade possible.

Trade
The exchange of goods and services.
Barter
Direct exchange without money.
Supply chain management
Coordination of activities from sourcing to delivery.
Logistics
The movement and handling of goods within that system.

Technology platforms and business growth

The discussion moved from those foundations to platforms in the modern economy and opportunities for companies to grow. The event poster placed the talk within a broader discussion of changes in globalization; its abstract is available in the event record.

The Flexport example

Ko considered banks, merchants and logistics providers before explaining Flexport as a response to their challenges. The notes establish this relationship between participants and the platform, but do not document specific performance improvements, a detailed financing mechanism or a quantitative comparison.

A separate company reference in Resources describes Flexport’s workspace for shipments, orders and inventory. That description provides background beyond the supplied lecture notes.
