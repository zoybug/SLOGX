Who drives, who responds?

Compare feature roles and an operating boundary.

The reflections often describe a transfer of responsibility. SAE’s six levels run from 0 to 5 and classify a driving feature, not a company’s reputation. “L2+” is an industry label, not a separate SAE level. The taxonomy is not a safety certificate.

Feature roles · brief paraphrases of the SAE taxonomy
LevelNameRole while engaged
L0No driving automationDriver performs the task; warnings or brief interventions may assist.
L1Driver assistanceSustained steering OR speed support; driver supervises.
L2Partial driving automationSustained steering AND speed support; driver supervises.
L3Conditional driving automationADS performs the task in its domain; a fallback-ready user must respond when required.
L4High driving automationADS performs the task and fallback within its domain; no driver takeover is required.
L5Full driving automationADS performs the task and fallback across driver-manageable on-road conditions.

Change the feature and the conditions.

This constructed comparison assumes a valid feature is engaged within its stated operational design domain (ODD), then considers an approaching boundary. An ODD may limit roads, speed, weather, lighting or other conditions.

At L3, a fallback-ready user is expected to respond to a request to intervene and relevant evident failures. At L4, the ADS handles fallback, which may end in a minimal risk condition. The exact maneuver depends on the situation; remote assistance and a mapped zone alone do not establish a level.

A hands-free display or a vehicle with no visible driver does not establish driverless capability. Tesla’s FSD (Supervised) documentation explicitly retains active driver supervision. Consumer driver support and a separately designed robotaxi service require their own feature-level evidence.

From sensing to control

Follow the five-step cognition loop and the development cycle.

01

INPUT → DECISION → CHECK

Choose sensors for their failure modes.

The class compares camera detail, radar range/relative motion, LiDAR geometry and short-range ultrasonic sensing, with GNSS/IMU supporting vehicle state. Weather, lighting, occlusion, calibration, compute and cost shape performance. Fusion can add complementary evidence; adding sensors alone does not prove safe operation.

Keep development separate from live control.

The data flywheel collects relevant events, checks and labels data, trains candidate models, validates them and releases controlled updates. Fleet experience does not automatically rewrite a production controller after each trip. AVOps also covers monitoring, maintenance and incident response.

Data volume needs coverage.

The lecture contrasts vision-focused, multisensor, ecosystem and supplier strategies. Students connect those strategies with clean data, hardware integration and validation. A large fleet or a high TOPS figure does not rank safety across different routes, software and test conditions.

The five steps simplify a system that runs as a repeated feedback loop; perception and prediction remain uncertain. End-to-end learning and modular stacks may organize these functions differently. Generative simulation can extend scenario coverage, while real-world checks must address its domain gap.

Traffic flow and service capacity

Test two explicit assumptions before claiming efficiency.

One reflection discusses ring-road and grid models, local versus global control and mixed traffic. Other submissions turn capacity into an operating plan for campus shuttles, freight convoys and delivery hubs. Start with an explicit model, then identify what it leaves out.

How much does the headway assumption matter?

For a constructed single-lane stream, assume human-driven vehicles have a 2.0-second crossing headway. Change the share and assumed crossing headway of automated vehicles.

VS HUMAN-ONLY ASSUMPTION

Calculation: h̄ = (1 − p) × 2.0 + p × hAV; q = 3600/h̄ vehicles/hour. This averages assigned crossing headways, assumes continuous demand and omits pair-specific following behavior, disturbances, lane changes, signals and safety constraints. It is not a microscopic simulation, a validated capacity forecast or a recommended following gap. A larger AV share helps this calculation only when its assumed headway is shorter.

Control strategy changes the result.

A ring-road experiment shows how a controlled vehicle can dampen stop-and-go waves under that experiment’s conditions. It does not imply every added AV improves a city network. A simulation must specify demand, behavior, control, routing and evaluation metrics.

2025 STUDENT CONTENT / 05 OF 05LECTURE 06

Earn trust through operation

Carry student experience, access and evidence into deployment.

PERSONAL EXPERIENCE

Smoothness belongs in the review.

One student recalls abrupt braking on a shuttle used for commuting. Keep that experience as a question about comfort, false detections and operational behavior; it is not a fleet-level failure rate. Measure unnecessary braking alongside near misses, injuries, response and passenger feedback.

ACCESS + PARTICIPATION

Design for the entire journey.

Reports raise affordability, smartphone and network access, fragmented operator systems, wheelchair access and multilingual support. Booking access is only one step: boarding, assistance, accessible stops, emergency communication and a recovery option also matter.

PATROL + HUMAN AUTHORITY

A mobile sensor needs a defined role.

Patrol-focused submissions ask about false alarms, bias and the authority to intervene. Separate driving performance from threat classification. Specify who reviews an alert and authorizes a response; an empty cabin or a product table does not establish either capability.

TRUST + EXPLANATIONS

A readable account needs evidence.

Some reflections emphasize empathy and confidence in a shared city; others propose LLM passenger explanations. A fluent narrative may not describe the actual cause of a control action. Check it against event logs, communicate uncertainty and retain a path to human support.

ENERGY + URBAN SPACE

Measure the network effect.

Reflections imagine electric fleets, smoother travel and less parking. Electrification, shared use and automation are separate design choices. Compare energy sources, occupancy, empty repositioning, vehicle distance and replaced trips before claiming cleaner or less congested streets.

WORKFORCE + OPERATIONS

Include the people who keep it running.

Reports discuss changing driving jobs and new work in fleet maintenance, monitoring, simulation and safety. A service budget includes those activities, charging, insurance and incident support. Assess staffing and training rather than assuming an autonomous fleet has no labor costs.

APPLICATION BOUNDARIES

Passenger, freight and civic services differ.

Students connect robotaxis with port automation, mines, last-mile delivery, sanitation and campus patrol. A structured site can simplify some interactions but still contains people, equipment, changing conditions and failure modes. Validate the service in its own domain.

Build a pilot that can answer a question.

Define the domain, hazards, fallback, maintenance, incident response and change controls. Compare collision outcomes per exposure, service reliability, uncomfortable braking, remote interventions, total operating costs and access with appropriate baselines. Test privacy, data retention, message integrity and recovery from network loss.

Simulation coverage and disengagement counts describe parts of a test program; they do not directly rank different operators. Waymo’s safety materials offer a vendor example whose methods, operating area, exposure and comparator should be inspected.
