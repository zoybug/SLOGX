# Course Atlas editorial style

Write for students encountering a logistics topic for the first time, while
offering clear routes into technical detail. Use the voice of an educator who
explains a problem, shows an example and helps the reader reason about it.

## Shared section and activity names

| Role | Reader-facing name | Related controls |
|---|---|---|
| Lecture introduction and outline | Overview | — |
| Anonymous reflection views and associated teaching comparisons | Student synthesis | Explore student synthesis; Contents; Previous; Next; Full notes and assumptions |
| Related readings and source links | Resources | Related reading |
| Multiple-choice practice activity | Quiz | Next question; See quiz result; Restart quiz; Quiz complete |
| Provenance and inclusion details | About this synthesis | Expandable note |
| Archive page linking the lectures | 2025 lectures | Not yet available for an unpublished lecture |

Use the same name in navigation, headings, accessible labels, buttons and status
messages. Do not call the quiz a challenge, lab or decision lab. Worked models
can still be described as examples or exercises because they perform a different
role from the multiple-choice quiz.

Keep official syllabus lecture titles in Lecture details and sources, the archive's
course-details disclosure and browser title. A shorter hero heading introduces the
topic. Use the same abbreviated lecture label throughout the register and lecture rail:
Digital supply chains; Smart transportation; Intelligent warehousing; Urban drone
logistics; High-precision positioning; Autonomous driving; Container terminals.

## Information order

Begin with the lecture’s central question. Give enough context to explain why it
matters, then a short outline of what students will explore. A useful panel
sequence is process → concrete comparisons or exercises → student questions →
evaluation. Adapt it when the topic needs a different order.

Each synthesis view presents one takeaway, two supporting points and a useful
chart, comparison or model. Full notes retain the original explanations and
qualifications. Keep a short assumption beside the model when it affects the
reader's interpretation; put detailed assumptions in its full notes. Source
counts and document reconciliation belong in About this synthesis.

## Language and headings

Use concrete actors and actions: a dispatcher assigns a route, a station checks a
package, a filter weights a measurement. Prefer these to clusters such as
“connected, predictive, adaptive intelligence” without a worked explanation.

Use sentence case for headings and controls. Omit terminal periods from headings.
Decorative topic markers may
remain uppercase. Use question headings when they introduce an actual question;
avoid alternating metaphors and technical labels for the same function.

Keep one main idea per paragraph. Split dense lists of concepts into a clear
relationship or a concrete example. Cut repetition when a second statement adds
no explanation, example, condition or consequence. Retain necessary mathematics
and precise terminology; clarity does not require making every passage shorter.

Expand unfamiliar abbreviations at their first meaningful use. Examples include
warehouse management system (WMS), warehouse control system (WCS), hidden Markov
model (HMM), global navigation satellite system (GNSS), inertial navigation system
(INS), operational design domain (ODD) and automated driving system (ADS). Keep
acronyms correctly capitalized in topic labels.

Use “students,” “reflections” and “submissions” according to their meaning. A
student can supply more than one submission. Avoid substituting one denominator
for another. Refer to S.F. Holding consistently rather than alternating with SF.

## Sources and interpretation

Distinguish lecture material, student perspective and editorial teaching additions.
Do not imply that an editorial model is the professor’s named framework, a student
concept is a deployed product or one perspective represents the whole class.

Keep counts, source dates, citations and technical assumptions accurate. Identify
historical announcements by date. Describe manufacturer material as an explanation
or claim, with performance evaluated under stated conditions. Do not silently
replace source uncertainty with a fluent definitive sentence.

Use a short source note where a qualification changes the reader’s interpretation.
Detailed reconciliation belongs in the private evidence record or an expandable
source note. Existing link-check dates are historical records; update them only
after a new check has actually been performed.

## Quizzes and interactive copy

Write a realistic decision question with one defensible answer. Incorrect choices
should reflect plausible misunderstandings, rather than irrelevant colors, fonts
or logos. Explain the reasoning in one or two direct sentences and link a relevant
resource. Keep correct answer positions aligned with the answer key.

Use plain progress and result messages: “Question 3 of 12 · Score: 2” and “Quiz
complete · Score: 10 of 12.” State the result directly and provide a useful next
step. Internal review links should open the relevant panel in the same page;
external resources may open a new tab.

When panels are reordered, update contents links, panel numbering, accessible
labels and legacy hash behavior together. Review the rendered text on desktop and
mobile, including hidden controls, feedback and completion states.
