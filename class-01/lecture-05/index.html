Lecture 05 / 20 October 2026

High-Accuracy Positioning for ITS

A delivery driver can see a convincing blue dot on the wrong street. This lecture traces how satellite receivers estimate a location, how inertial sensors carry the estimate through gaps, and how a road map helps identify the route a vehicle took.

LecturerProf. Kai Zhang

Kai Zhang is an associate professor at Tsinghua Shenzhen International Graduate School. His official profile lists teaching in multisensor fusion and intelligent transportation, and research in information fusion, statistical signal processing and high-accuracy position and attitude estimation.

The assignment

Student work

Write one page on the part of the lecture that interested you most. Suggested starting points included satellite errors, GNSS and inertial fusion, visual or LiDAR odometry, smartphone drift, satellite coverage and local LiDAR suppliers.

How the work was read

The synthesis covers 33 positioning reports. Sensor comparisons, applications and explanations of error are paraphrased from the submitted work. The summaries omit names, identifiers and grades.

Read the 2025 class on high-accuracy positioning

A dot can be wrong in space or out of date

A reflected signal can put a courier across the street. A perfectly good fix can also become stale while the courier keeps moving.

Read the analysis

Reports often began with a familiar symptom. A rider’s pin appears across a busy street, the pickup moves between parallel roads, or the app keeps showing a vehicle where it used to be. Students explained how a satellite signal bouncing off a building travels a longer path, distorting the distance inferred by the receiver. Others focused on timing. A location recorded correctly at the previous update can still mislead someone waiting now. These explanations point to different remedies. Showing when the last update arrived helps a dispatcher judge its age. Comparing the fix with nearby streets and sensor readings helps investigate a misplaced pin.

What carries the estimate through a tunnel?

Students used a tunnel to explain when to trust satellite observations and when to rely on measured motion.

Read the analysis

Students traced the calculation as a vehicle entered a tunnel. Before entry, satellite observations gave the vehicle a position reference. Inside, an inertial measurement unit could still sense acceleration and turns, so the system could continue estimating motion. A persistent sensor bias also accumulates during that calculation. The longer the outage, the less certain the resulting position becomes. Some reports used the Kalman filter’s prediction and correction steps to explain how a reliable observation can pull the estimate back when reception returns. Others asked a more practical question: how much should an operator trust the first fix outside a tunnel lined with reflective buildings?

Choosing between two plausible roads

Reports treated the driven road as something to infer from a sequence of fixes, especially around flyovers and parallel streets.

Read the analysis

Students described the mistake a navigation display can make when the closest road to one observation happens to be a parallel street. A previous fix, the direction of travel and the connections between road segments provide evidence about which route was possible. Some reports followed the hidden Markov model from the lecture. The road segment is hidden, each position fix is an observation, and a sequence of plausible transitions can support one route more strongly than another. Simple examples made that reasoning easier to follow. A junction can settle an ambiguity that several evenly spaced fixes on two straight roads leave unresolved.

Choosing accuracy for the job

The right question changes with the service. A pickup needs the right entrance. A road charge needs a defensible record of the road used.

Read the analysis

Reports connected positioning to the next decision. A dispatch system needs a recent location to choose a nearby vehicle. A road-pricing system may need enough evidence to tell a charged road from the one beside it and to check a disputed fee. Some students investigated local reference stations, satellite constellations and correction services. Others examined what LiDAR suppliers and mapping companies actually provide. These examples broadened the discussion beyond an accuracy figure. A correction network can help with errors shared by receivers over an area, while reflections from a particular building still depend on the local street. Coverage, equipment and the operating setting all shape the choice.

Teaching example

Try the decision

How far behind is the pickup pin?

Dispatch receives a vehicle position after a delay. Change the speed and the age of the fix to estimate how far the vehicle has travelled since the position was recorded.

At 5 m/s, a vehicle travels 60 m in the 12 seconds since the last fix.

Assume constant speed on a straight road. The original fix has its own measurement uncertainty.

Self-check

Decision quiz

Twelve short scenarios. Choose a response and check the reasoning behind it.

A rider’s pin jumps across the road beside glass towers. Which observation would most directly support a multipath explanation?
Answer and reasoning

The receiver gets a delayed reflection of a satellite signal.

A reflected signal travels farther than the direct path. Treating that longer trip as a direct range can bias the estimated position.

A vehicle loses satellite reception inside a tunnel. What should a fleet dashboard allow for while it uses inertial readings?
Answer and reasoning

Position uncertainty can grow as motion estimates accumulate sensor bias.

An inertial unit measures motion. Integrating those readings bridges an outage, but small biases accumulate.

Two parallel roads lie close to several noisy fixes. What additional evidence helps identify the route?
Answer and reasoning

Whether the sequence of fixes follows connected, plausible turns.

Map matching can use road connectivity and motion across several observations to compare candidate routes.

A technically accurate fix arrived 20 seconds ago. What should a dispatcher check before using it for a pickup?
Answer and reasoning

The age of the fix and how far the vehicle could have moved.

The vehicle can move after an accurate measurement. Freshness and measurement accuracy answer different questions.

Two fresh estimates disagree just after a van exits a tunnel. The satellite receiver flags poor signal quality. What should a fusion filter consider before correcting its predicted position?
Answer and reasoning

The uncertainty of both the motion prediction and the satellite measurement.

The correction depends on the relative uncertainty. An unreliable measurement should generally have less influence than an otherwise comparable reliable one.

An onboard unit bills for use of a toll road running beside a free street. A driver disputes the charge. Which record would best support a review?
Answer and reasoning

Time-stamped observations, the road sequence assigned and the uncertainty around that assignment.

The dispute concerns which road the vehicle used. Reconstructing the assignment requires observations and the route interpretation behind the charge.

Roadworks open a new connection between two streets. A fleet’s map matcher rejects every route through it even though the fixes are consistent. What should the team inspect first?
Answer and reasoning

The date and connectivity of the road-network data.

A route can be impossible in an outdated map even when the vehicle followed it in the real world.

A reference-station correction service improves results on an open survey site. Before promising the same improvement beside tall buildings, what should a fleet test?
Answer and reasoning

Errors on the actual delivery streets, including reflections local to the vehicle.

A reference station can estimate errors shared across an area. A reflection near a particular vehicle still depends on its immediate surroundings.

Two proposed routes have equally good Fréchet distances from a recorded trace. What does that tie tell an analyst?
Answer and reasoning

The geometric score alone cannot distinguish these candidates.

Fréchet distance compares curve geometry. Similar shapes can give equally good scores, leaving other evidence to settle the route.

A fleet down-samples a trace until several intersections fall between successive observations. Why might its road assignment change?
Answer and reasoning

Several different connected paths may fit the same remaining observations.

Sparse samples remove evidence about turns between fixes. Alternative paths can then explain the observed points.

Two inertial units begin a test from the same known position. One has a small persistent acceleration bias. What is the likely effect during an interval with no external corrections?
Answer and reasoning

Its inferred velocity and displacement can diverge as the bias is integrated.

Acceleration contributes to inferred velocity and then to displacement. A persistent measurement offset can accumulate across both steps.

In a hidden Markov map matcher, two roads are similarly close to the latest fix. What does the transition term add to the comparison?
Answer and reasoning

How plausible it is to move from a preceding candidate to each current road.

The observation term relates a fix to a candidate road. The transition term relates successive candidate states and helps compare complete sequences.
