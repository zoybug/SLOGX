Field visit / 13 October 2026

Inside SF Express in Shenzhen

At Qianhai headquarters the class saw the network on a map. At the Bao’an Air Cargo Transit Hub, it could follow a parcel from a truck to a scanner, a chute and an onward flight.

Visit hostProf. Wai Kin (Victor) Chan

Wai Kin (Victor) Chan is a professor at Tsinghua University and the course instructor. His research includes simulation, optimization and statistical analysis, with applications in transportation and other complex systems.

Field notes

The written account of the October visit follows the class from headquarters to the air hub.

Two views of the same delivery network

At Qianhai, the exhibition put air, ground and international services on one map.

Read the visit account

The headquarters tour traced how SF developed from an express carrier into a network spanning air freight, international shipping and supply chain services. Staff described employee training and university partnerships, while an engineer discussed route planning and network optimization. Those conversations gave the class a question to take to its second stop. What information does a worker at one hub need to keep the next connection moving?

How a parcel finds its chute

At Bao’an, six-sided scanners read labels as parcels moved along the conveyor.

Read the visit account

The class followed parcels arriving by truck and passing through scanning points. Reading from several angles reduced the need to turn each box by hand. The parcel record identified the next destination, and a cross-belt sorter sent the parcel to the corresponding chute. Robotic trolleys moved freight between areas. Every movement depended on a legible label, a usable record and a destination that matched the next leg.

A sorted parcel still needs a flight

Control displays connected parcel flow on the floor with aircraft connections.

Read the visit account

Staff showed the visitors how they monitored parcel movement and flight connections, with warnings when a bottleneck emerged. The tour also covered screening and the preparation of air cargo containers. A box that finishes sorting still has to reach its assigned connection in time and meet its handling requirements. One way to assess that process is to count the parcels that make the required connection alongside the parcels that pass a scanner.

Different freight needs different handling

International and temperature-sensitive shipments had additional requirements.

Read the visit account

Staff pointed out customs-controlled areas for international parcels and cold-chain facilities for goods such as fresh food and medicine. Two boxes can pass the same scanner and then need different space, equipment and release checks. The visit account records the process the class saw. Comparing transit times or temperatures would require shipment records and a defined period.
